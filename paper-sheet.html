Step 2 · Read your band

BandScoreWhat it means for that pillar
Depleted3–11This pillar is running on empty and is now limiting the other three. It is not a motivation problem — treat it as the first thing to repair.
Strained12–17Holding, but with no margin. One bad week knocks it over. What is usually missing is rhythm and a written system, not effort.
Steady18–23Functioning and not blocking you. The jump to strong comes from direction and consistency, not from more intensity.
Strong24–30A genuine asset. Protect the one habit carrying it, and use the surplus on your weakest pillar.

Step 3 · Read the gap between your pillars

Gap (highest − lowest)What it says about you
0–4 pointsBalanced. Your whole self runs at about the same level. Lifting one pillar deliberately will pull the others up with it.

Suggestions & Group Discussion

Work on your lowest pillar first · 30 days

Step 4 · Improvement actions for your growth edge

Find your lowest pillar below. Do the first two actions. Ignore the rest until those two are habits.

Body — Physical Self

Low here usually means irregularity, not damage — sleep, meals and movement that move by hours from day to day.
  1. Fix your wake-up time first, not your sleep time. Same alarm seven days a week.
  2. No screen for the last 30 minutes before bed — phone across the room, on charge.
  3. Move 20 minutes daily, non-negotiable. A brisk walk counts.
  4. Eat a real breakfast within an hour of waking.
  5. Water before tea or coffee each morning.

Mind — Intellectual Self

Low here is usually input overload, not low intelligence. Your attention is being spent by other people's design.
  1. One 45-minute block daily with the phone in another room.
  2. Read 15 pages a day of something not on your syllabus.
  3. Write decisions down before making them: options, what you want, what you fear.
  4. Teach one thing you learned this week to someone else.
  5. Unfollow ten accounts that leave you agitated; add three that leave you thinking.

Wealth — Material Self

A low score here is rarely about how much you have — it is about how much of it you can see and steer.
  1. Track every rupee for 30 days. Do not budget yet — just see it.
  2. Save a fixed amount the day money arrives, however small.
  3. Write your twelve-month target in one sentence: skill, savings figure or position.
  4. Build one paid-market skill 45 minutes, three times a week.
  5. Audit your time like money for one week.

Spirit — Purposeful Self

This is not about religion. It is about whether anything you did this week actually mattered to you.
  1. Write your three core values in your own words, then check last week against them.
  2. Five minutes of silence each morning before touching the phone.
  3. Journal three lines nightly: what mattered, what drained me, what I carry forward.
  4. Do one thing weekly that helps someone with no benefit to you.
  5. Name the one compromise you already know is out of line with your values — set a date to end it.

Step 5 · Discuss with your group — circle of 3, twelve minutes

Round 1 · 90 sec eachSay your growth edge out loud and one honest sentence about why it scored lowest.
Round 2 · 60 sec eachListeners reflect back: “What I heard is…” No fixing, no judging.
Round 3 · 90 sec eachState your 30-day action. The group writes it down and picks a date to ask you.
  1. Which pillar did your table collectively score lowest? Why might a whole group of students share the same weak pillar?
  2. Was your lowest score a surprise, or something you already knew and had been avoiding?
  3. Where were you tempted to score yourself higher than the truth — and what does that say about how you want to be seen?
  4. Whose 30-day action sounds most achievable? Steal the structure of it for your own.
  5. Pick a date three weeks from today. Who in this circle will message you to ask whether you kept your commitment?
